Duplicates, by content

Size buckets first, then a hash of the first 64 KB, then a full read only for the survivors. Files that merely share a name or a size are rejected before anything expensive happens.

Identical files that diverge in their last kilobyte still get caught.

Find what's old, not just what's big

The Age Map splits your bytes by last-modified date and surfaces Big & Untouched — large files you have not opened in over a year, ranked by how much space they would give back.

When most of a drive was written last month, a log scale shows the shape of the tail instead of one bar and a flat line.

Nothing leaves without you

DiskLens has no delete button that acts on its own. Every removal goes through the same three steps.

01

Stage

Pressing + puts an item on a list. Nothing on disk changes. Stage a whole duplicate group, a cache folder, or every file over 100 MB you have not opened in a year.

02

Review

The Cleanup page shows every staged path and its size in one place, so you can see the whole set at once instead of approving things one dialog at a time.

03

Move to Trash

Only then does anything move — and it goes to the Trash, not into the void. Empty it when you are satisfied, or put something back.

System paths are refused outright

Home folders, /System, /usr, keychains, mail and messages are excluded from staging before the question is even asked.

Run it

Python 3 and a browser. No packages, no virtualenv, no build.

$ python3 server.py
DiskLens running at http://127.0.0.1:8765/

Scans in parallel

The walker runs on a pool of threads. scandir and stat both release the GIL, so the per-file syscall that dominates a scan overlaps across cores.

Holds millions of files

The tree is a set of flat arrays rather than nested objects, which keeps a two-million-file scan in the low hundreds of megabytes.

Prunes what crosses the wire

The browser only ever receives the levels it is drawing, largest children first, with the remainder folded into a bucket that keeps the totals honest.
